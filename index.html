Aerodynamics

Forces on an aircraft

Aircraft performance is strongly influenced by lift, drag, thrust and weight. The wing generates aerodynamic lift as air moves around its geometry.

Lift equation
L = ½ ρ V² S CL
ρ density · V velocity · S wing area · CL lift coefficient

Aircraft Structures

Loads, stress and structural integrity

Aircraft structures carry aerodynamic, inertial and landing loads. Structural analysis helps ensure adequate strength, stiffness and safety.

Propulsion

Producing thrust

Aircraft propulsion systems convert chemical or electrical energy into useful propulsive force. Gas turbines use compression, combustion and expansion to produce high-speed exhaust flow.

Simple thrust relation
T ≈ ṁ (Ve − V0)
ṁ mass flow rate · Ve exit velocity · V0 flight velocity

Flight Mechanics

Understanding aircraft motion

Flight mechanics studies aircraft equilibrium, performance and motion. The aircraft is commonly described using longitudinal and lateral-directional motion.

Load factor
n = L / W

Flight Control Systems

From pilot input to aircraft response

Flight control systems transmit pilot or automatic-control commands to aerodynamic control surfaces such as elevators, ailerons and rudders.

Basic feedback concept
e(t) = r(t) − y(t)
e error · r reference input · y measured output

Aircraft Materials

Materials for strength and weight efficiency

Aircraft materials are selected considering strength-to-weight ratio, fatigue, corrosion, temperature capability, manufacturability and cost.

Specific strength
Specific strength = Strength / Density
